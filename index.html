01 — Systems · Live

Roll call used to take ten minutes.
This takes one scan.

RollOne checks in a whole lecture hall at once. The hard part isn’t the face model. It’s 120 phones hitting the server in the same fifteen seconds, and students who’d happily mark a friend present from the canteen.

    Illustrative replay. The system is sized to check in a 120-student class in under 25 s.

    1. 01

      Scan the rotating QR

      HMAC-SHA256 tokens that rotate on the projector, pushed over Server-Sent Events. A photo sent to a friend is already stale.

    2. 02

      Type the PIN you can see

      A line-of-sight PIN only people in the room can read.

    3. 03

      Be inside the room

      A Haversine geofence around the classroom.

    4. 04

      Prove it’s you

      A FaceNet check behind a micro-batching queue, with staggered upload slots so the start of class doesn’t become a thundering herd.

    145ms

    per face, down from 145 ms. Up to 16 faces per forward pass: 2.2× throughput.

    0.0s

    median QR scan on the production server, 120-student k6 burst (1.8 s p95).

    0tests

    pass with Redis on and off. The load test found an OpenCV thread-safety crash in production. It’s fixed.

    0double marks

    by design: PostgreSQL UNIQUE constraints + ON CONFLICT upserts make concurrent check-ins idempotent.

    02 — Retrieval · Live

    Legal answers that
    show their sources.

    OpenForge answers questions about Indian law and cites the exact section. Before I changed a line of retrieval, I wrote 120 test questions. They caught a chunking bug that had quietly indexed 49 of the 358 sections of the BNS.

    03 — Research · MVI Lab, NIT Jamshedpur

    Fewer signals.
    Clearer answers.

    A brain–computer interface usually needs a 22-electrode cap. I designed GGCS, a graph-convolution method that picks the channels and classifies at the same time. It cuts the cap to five electrodes with competitive accuracy on BCI Competition IV-2a.

    Co-authored paper under review, Neural Computing and Applications (Springer) · Supervisor Prof. K. K. Singh · Jul–Dec 2025
